# Contributing

The Enso Method is open source, but it isn't really open to contributions.

The method is opinionated on purpose. It is how I build software with AI agents, and each skill is tuned against the others and against real work, both for clients and on my own projects. Reviewing someone else's change properly means understanding the problem they hit, working out what else the change would affect, and usually a round of back-and-forth. I don't have the time to do that well, and doing it badly helps no one.

- **Fork it freely.** The [MIT License](LICENSE) lets you change it however you like, including at work and in commercial products. If the method doesn't fit how you work, a fork is the right answer. Please give a fork that changes the method its own name, rather than calling it The Enso Method.
- **Issues are welcome,** especially reports of a skill that misfires, contradicts another skill, or steers an agent wrong. Say what you asked the agent to do and what it did. I read them when I can, but I can't promise a reply or a fix.
- **Pull requests** are unlikely to be merged, so don't expect a review or a response. If you open one anyway, keep it small and explain the problem it solves. If I take the idea, I may write the change myself rather than merge yours.

If your team wants help adopting the method, that is what [EnsoDynamics](https://ensodynamics.com) does.

— Doug Kerwin
