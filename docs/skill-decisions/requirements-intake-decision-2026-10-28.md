# requirements-intake: a first step for raw source material (decision record, 2026-10-28)

Maintainer notes for `skills/requirements-intake/SKILL.md` and the matching lines in `using-the-enso-method`, `prd-writing-standards`, `initiative-breakdown`, `autonomous-prd-refinement`, `assumptions-document-writing`, `implementation-lifecycle` and `pre-commit-validation`, plus the README and the setup guide. This file is outside every skill directory and is not loaded by any skill.

## The problem

- **The method had no step for reading raw material.** `using-the-enso-method` says the method starts from the user's own material, however rough, and all of it is input to the PRD. `prd-writing-standards` is a format standard whose first step is the size check, and `initiative-breakdown` reads every input and merges it. Neither separates what stakeholders decided from discussion, exploration, reversed positions or text nobody with authority wrote.
- **Raw material is getting larger and less human.** Long meeting transcripts were already hard: a topic comes up three times and the answer changes. Users now also hand over the output of earlier AI attempts at the same work: plans, rule lists, issue lists and option pages, some answered and many not. Merged as is, that material reaches the stakeholder review as coined terms, single-case questions and conflicts the material settled elsewhere.

## Options considered

1. **Teach `prd-writing-standards` to sort its input.** Rejected: it would mix a reading procedure into a format standard, and `initiative-breakdown` would need the same procedure separately.
2. **Leave sorting to the user.** Rejected as the only answer: it works when the user knows to do it, and the method should not depend on that.
3. **Chosen:** a separate skill, `requirements-intake`, that runs first when the material is raw and writes one record the next skill works from. It reads any kind of source the way a long kickoff transcript is best read (extract everything, cross-check by topic, then separate decisions from discussion), and leaves PRD writing to `prd-writing-standards`.

## Decisions and why

- **Quote, don't summarize.** Every item in the record carries the stakeholders' verbatim words with their source; a one-sentence label is the only synthesis. Summaries lose qualifiers, examples and the stakeholders' own terms, which are exactly what later documents get wrong.
- **Whose words count is explicit.** A stakeholder's choice among options someone else wrote is a decision, recorded with the option text labelled as not theirs. Text nobody with authority wrote is context at most, and unanswered options are nothing.
- **A later statement wins only when it knowingly replaces the earlier one.** Within one meeting or thread, the discussion moved on to it without pushback, or someone revised their own position; across sources, its author knew the earlier position or has authority over it. Otherwise two people's positions are a conflict whatever their dates, so a disagreement between people who never heard each other is not settled by the calendar.
- **The record is checked mechanically where it can be.** Quotes are looked up as exact strings in the sources, and the sources are searched for decision words, so the check does not depend on a model rereading a pile it could not hold.
- **The PRD starts in a new chat.** A chat that has just read the pile writes from the pile; the record only does its job when the next skill reads it instead.
- **Conflicts are kept, not handed back.** The record quotes both sides and says what would settle it. The PRD's 95% rule and the refinement stages take it from there, so the stakeholder sees it only as a researched assumption, consistent with the method's rule that the user is not asked about requirements again.
- **Extraction may be split; judgment may not.** Large material can be extracted by subagents returning verbatim quotes only, but deciding the latest position, the conflicts and the decisions runs in one context over all of it, and the record is never stitched from pieces written separately.
- **Two parts: bring the material in, then write the record.** What was received and what the skill wrote are different kinds of thing, and a reader, human or AI, has to be able to tell them apart at a glance. Part 1 copies material in and is committed on its own; Part 2 writes the one record. Part 1 decides only what the repository keeps; Part 2 still reads everything, including what was left out.
- **One root for material received from the business: `source-material/`, at the repository root.** It already held stakeholder feedback, handoff packages and discovery findings. It sits outside `docs/` because what arrives is not all documentation: prototypes and earlier attempts arrive as code. An intake gets `source-material/{name}/`, holding the record and `received/`.
- **What comes in is decided by value, not by who wrote it.** The stakeholders' raw words come in whole (every transcript, thread and document a stakeholder wrote), so the original is there when a question comes up later. Code handed over comes in as reference. Everything else is whittled down to the documents that earn a place: first anything carrying a stakeholder's answer, then current documents that give something the stakeholders' material does not. An AI-written document is not excluded for being AI-written. The trouble with a large pile is volume, age and not knowing what is still right, and an AI-written document that clears the test is useful context, though it still decides nothing ("Whose words count" above). Each one brought in needs a one-line statement of what it gives, so the call is checkable. The record quotes only material in the repository.
- **Everything brought in must belong in a Git repository.** It stays in the history for good, so secrets, personal and customer data, large media and binaries, generated output, and an earlier attempt's tooling stay out whatever their category. Agent instruction files are named because an AI tool working in the repository can load one as its own instructions.
- **Received material is quarantined three ways.** It sits under `source-material/`; each file that can carry a comment gets a fixed reference-only note at the top (fixed so it can be searched for); and code sits in a `reference-only-code-{what}/` directory, so the path alone says it is not the application. Code files keep their own names, since renaming many files would make the code harder to follow and the path already carries the warning. The application's build, test, lint and packaging tools are configured to skip the folder, since many collect files anywhere in the repository.
- **Material already in the repository is not moved.** Someone put it there on purpose, and code there may be in use; the record cites it by path. Unendorsed text sitting where requirements are read from is listed and pointed out to the user, who decides whether to move it.
- **Missing names, roles and dates are expected, never invented.** Within one meeting or thread the order is known even without speaker names, and a position the discussion visibly settled on stands. Where order, speaker or authority is too uncertain to say one position replaced another, the two are recorded as conflicting rather than guessed.
- **Run before the first draft, at a very high effort.** The skill runs once per piece of work and later skills read only its record, so a decision it misses is invisible from then on. That makes this the place to spend effort, which the operator sets when starting the session, since a skill cannot change it. The guidance says "very high" rather than naming a level, because the levels differ between tools, and stops short of an exhaustive multi-agent mode, which this work does not need.
- **The loop is in the review, not the extraction.** Extraction and the topic sweep already read everything twice, and the quote and decision-word checks run by search. What a single pass gets wrong is judgment (a missed decision, a reversal read backwards), so the record goes through fresh-eyes `deliverable-review` rounds until one finds no High, at most three.
- **The record's sections fit any kind of material.** It opens with what kind of work and material this is, then the problem and goals, who uses it and how things work today, before the decisions. A replacement leans on the current state, a pile of complaints on problems and real cases, early discovery on unresolved items. Empty sections are left out, and a section can be added for anything the material holds that fits none. Mockups, prototypes and documents brought in are listed under Reference Material, with what each gives, so the PRD's author knows to look at them.
- **Skipped for clean input.** A written brief or an email or two goes straight to `prd-writing-standards`, as before. The step is for raw material only, so most work is unaffected.
